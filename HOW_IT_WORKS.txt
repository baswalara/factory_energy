FACTORY ENERGY AGENT - HOW IT WORKS, IN SIMPLE ENGLISH
========================================================

Diagrams for everything below are in docs/architecture_and_flow.pdf (pie charts, expected-vs-actual
chart, two flowcharts and the architecture). Rebuild that PDF any time with:
    python scripts/make_docs_pdf.py


1. WHAT THE PRODUCT DOES
------------------------
A factory's electricity bill goes up or down every quarter. A finance manager wants to know WHY,
and an energy manager wants to know WHAT TO FIX.

This system answers both. You give it:
  - meter readings every 15 minutes (the main meter plus sub-meters such as compressors and hvac),
  - the tariff (price per kWh, which can change during a quarter),
  - production (units made per product family per day, shift pattern, holidays),
  - weather (daily temperature, cooling and heating degree-days).

It answers plain-English questions such as:
  - "Why did the bill go up?"            -> the change split into 5 causes that add up exactly
  - "Is energy above the baseline?"      -> expected energy vs actual energy, month by month
  - "What should we do first?"           -> actions with savings ranges, CO2e and payback
  - "How much energy is used when nothing is produced?"
  - "What if the tariff had not changed?"

The 5 causes are: tariff (price), production volume, product mix, weather, and waste (energy the
normal drivers cannot explain). They always add up to the total change, to the hundredth of a point.


2. THE BIG IDEA (THE WORKFLOW YOU ASKED FOR)
--------------------------------------------
  a) The PAST data lives in data/history: five quarters, 2025-Q3 to 2026-Q3.
  b) The user uploads a NEW quarter (four CSV files) through the web page, the API or a script.
  c) The CURRENT quarter (the newest complete one) is compared with the LATEST PAST quarter.
  d) A BASELINE is learned from the 4 quarters BEFORE the current one, using linear regression.
     The baseline answers: "Given what we produced and the weather, how much energy SHOULD we have used?"
  e) EXPECTED energy (from the baseline) is compared with ACTUAL energy (from the main meter).
  f) The bill change is split into the 5 causes, and any waste must be backed by a sub-meter before
     a machine is named.

Example with the shipped data:
  Current quarter 2026-Q3 is compared with 2026-Q2.
  The baseline learns from 2025-Q3, 2025-Q4, 2026-Q1 and 2026-Q2 (362 clean days).
  If you upload 2026-Q4, everything moves on by one quarter: 2026-Q4 vs 2026-Q3, and the baseline
  learns from 2025-Q4 to 2026-Q3. The current quarter is NEVER used to learn its own baseline,
  otherwise any new waste would be learned as "normal" and hidden.


3. THE MODELS WE USE
--------------------
There are two kinds of model. Only one of them is an AI language model, and it never makes a number.

3.1 The baseline: linear regression (the model that makes the numbers)
  Formula, for every day:
      expected kWh = base load
                   + extra kWh on a working day
                   + (kWh per unit of P_A x units of P_A) + ... for every product family
                   + (kWh per cooling degree-day x CDD)
                   + (kWh per heating degree-day x HDD)

  - Method: ordinary least squares (statsmodels OLS, plus numpy least squares).
  - Trained on: the daily main-meter kWh of the 4 history quarters. Days with big gaps in the meter
    data, days without production or weather rows, and days logged as production while the lines
    were idle are left out.
  - Physical rule: a rate per unit or per degree-day may not be negative. If the data says it is,
    that rate is fixed at zero and the answer says so.
  - Why linear: every term is one cause, so the effect of volume, mix and weather can be separated
    and added up exactly. A black-box model (gradient boosting, neural network) predicts well but
    cannot say how much of the change was weather and how much was product mix.
  - How good it is on the shipped data: R-squared 0.838; out-of-sample error CV(RMSE) 12.7% per
    day (each week is predicted by a model that never saw that week); bias NMBE 0.0%.
  - Uncertainty: the model is refitted 200 times on resampled history days. The spread gives the
    low-high range shown next to each number.

3.2 The waste check (simple rules, no AI)
  For each sub-meter (compressors, hvac, furnace, lighting, pumps), look at its load while the
  production lines are stopped. Compare it with that meter's own pattern in the history quarters,
  after adjusting for weather. If the last 28 days are more than 15% AND more than 3 standard
  deviations above that pattern, the meter is flagged. That is the only way a machine gets named.
  On the shipped data the compressors are flagged (idle load about 73 kW above normal since early
  July 2026). HVAC is cleared: its rise is explained by the hottest quarter in the data.

3.3 The language model (LLM): Google Gemini
  - Provider: Google Gemini API (the key you gave is stored in the local .env file, which Git and
    Docker ignore).
  - Model in use: gemini-3.5-flash-lite.
    We first tried gemini-3.8-flash (the code's default for Gemini). At the time of testing it
    returned "high demand" errors (503) and timed out, so the backend was switched to
    gemini-3.5-flash-lite, which answered in about 2 seconds per call.
  - Before this, no key was used at all: the app ran with MOCK_LLM=true (keyword rules and a fixed
    text template).
  - What the LLM does (only two small jobs):
      1. Label a question when the keyword rules are not sure (for example "whats eating our power
         at night?" -> idle_waste).
      2. Rewrite the answer draft into short, plain sentences for a manager.
  - What the LLM NEVER does: calculate, choose numbers, call tools, or decide who is to blame.
  - Safety net: after the LLM writes, code checks every number in the text against the computed
    facts. One wrong number -> it is asked to rewrite once. Wrong again -> a fixed template is used.
    If Gemini is down or slow (20 second limit), the template is used and the numbers are still right.
  - Switch provider or model by editing .env: LLM_PROVIDER = gemini | openai | anthropic | ollama,
    LLM_MODEL = model name, MOCK_LLM = true to run with no LLM at all.


4. HOW THE CODE WORKS, STEP BY STEP
-----------------------------------
4.1 Getting data in
  app/loader.py      Reads a CSV, checks the required columns, rejects bad rows (and counts them),
                     collapses duplicates, and saves into the DuckDB database. A file is recognised
                     by the start of its name (meter_15min_..., tariff_..., production_..., weather_...).
  app/store.py       The DuckDB database file: the four tables, saved answers ("traces") and small
                     metadata. A corrected tariff replaces old prices only for the dates it covers.

4.2 Cleaning
  app/cleaning.py    Puts every meter on one 15-minute grid. Negative readings and impossible spikes
                     are treated as meter resets. Gaps up to 1 hour are interpolated; longer gaps are
                     filled with the typical value for that time of day. Builds one row per day with
                     kWh, units per product, degree-days and whether the day was really a working day
                     (a holiday logged as a shift is caught here).
                     It also decides which quarters are COMPLETE: at least 80% of the days must have
                     real meter readings, production, weather AND tariff. Filled-in values never count.
                     A quarter with some data but not enough is PARTIAL (a quarter still in progress).

4.3 Prices
  app/tools/billing.py  Gives every 15-minute reading the price in force at that moment. It
                     understands dated tariff blocks (07:00-22:00 peak on weekdays, flat at weekends),
                     which is how the history tariff is written. Bill = sum of kWh x price.

4.4 The analysis tools (no AI in any of these files)
  app/tools/baseline.py  The linear regression above: fit, quality check, ranges, predictions.
  app/tools/expected.py  Expected vs actual energy for a quarter, by month, plus the biggest weekly excess.
  app/tools/bridge.py    The bill split into 5 causes:
                           tariff  = new energy at new prices minus new energy at the old average price
                           volume, mix, weather = the baseline's rates x the change in units, mix, degree-days
                           waste   = whatever is left
                         Because the model is linear, the waste slice equals
                         (actual - expected this quarter) - (actual - expected last quarter).
  app/tools/waste.py     The sub-meter waste check (section 3.2).
  app/tools/assets.py    Energy by sub-meter, and energy used while the lines are stopped.
  app/tools/savings.py   Turns each flagged problem into an action with kWh, money, CO2e and payback
                         ranges. Costs and recovery shares are stated assumptions in config.yaml.
  app/validation.py      8 data checks (sub-meters vs main meter, resets, timestamps, tariff coverage,
                         holidays, row counts, quarter completeness, baseline quality).

4.5 Answering a question (app/graph.py, one LangGraph flow with 6 steps)
  1. parse    Guard rules first: requests to invent numbers, skip the weather adjustment or blame a
              machine are refused; forecasts and other fuels are "not in the data". Then keyword rules
              label the question. The LLM is asked only if the rules are unsure.
  2. periods  Picks the current quarter and the latest past quarter (or the ones the user named).
              Says clearly if a quarter is missing or incomplete.
  3. analyse  Runs the tools for that label and collects "facts" (the only numbers allowed).
  4. write    The LLM rewrites the template draft into plain English (or the template is used as is).
  5. check    Every number in the text must match a fact. Retry once, then the template.
  6. respond  Returns JSON: answer type, summary, bridge, recommendations, assumptions, limitations,
              and a run_id. GET /trace/{run_id} shows every step, tool, number and timing behind it.

4.6 Where you use it
  app/api.py           FastAPI: POST /analyze, POST /ingest, GET /periods, GET /validate,
                       GET /trace/{id}, GET /health.
  ui/streamlit_app.py  Web page: upload a new quarter, see which quarters are loaded, ask questions,
                       see the bridge chart, expected-vs-actual charts, actions and the trace.
  start.bat            Windows starter: makes the Python environment, loads the history the first
                       time, starts the API and the web page, opens the browser.
                       (Written but NOT run here: you stopped that test. Run it once and check the
                       two windows it opens.)
  config.yaml          Every assumption in one place: 4 history quarters, 80% completeness,
                       6% sub-meter tolerance, emission factor 0.4 kg CO2e/kWh, action costs.


5. A WORKED EXAMPLE (REAL OUTPUT ON THE SHIPPED DATA)
-----------------------------------------------------
  Bill 2026-Q2: 249,597    Bill 2026-Q3: 325,113    Change: +30.26%
  Split (percentage points of the 2026-Q2 bill):
      tariff (price)       8.54   prices rose on 2026-05-15 and 2026-08-01
      weather              6.50   2026-Q3 was the hottest quarter in the data
      production volume    5.19   more units made
      product mix          3.96   more of the energy-heavy products P_C and P_D
      waste                6.07   range 4.0 to 8.5, backed by the compressor meter
      total               30.26   (the five add up exactly)
  Expected vs actual: the baseline expected 1,784,209 kWh in 2026-Q3; the meter shows
  1,864,705 kWh (+4.5%). In 2026-Q2 actual was 0.8% below expected.
  Top action: repair compressed-air leaks; payback 0.4 to 4.1 months (cost is an assumption).


6. WHAT WE CHANGED TO GET HERE
------------------------------
  - Baseline moved from "learn from the one previous quarter" to "learn from the 4 quarters before
    the current one" (falls back to fewer, and says so, when less history exists).
  - New expected-vs-actual check and question type ("Is energy above the baseline?").
  - Tariffs written as dated time blocks are priced correctly. This also fixed a bug where the
    "tariff unchanged" what-if repriced only off-peak hours.
  - New quarters can be uploaded file by file. A quarter is used only once meter, production,
    weather and tariff are all in. Partial quarters get only the expected-vs-actual check.
  - Waste is judged against each meter's weather-adjusted pattern over the same 4 history quarters.
  - Two new validation checks (quarter completeness and history; baseline quality).
  - Guard fixes: "skip the weather adjustment / just blame X / write that waste is 30%" are refused;
    an unknown meter ("the boiler") gets a question back; "write the finance brief" works.
  - Gemini connected; the LLM now rewrites a correct draft instead of reading raw data, which
    gave much clearer answers. Rules decide the label whenever they are sure.
  - Tests: 56 automated tests pass. The evaluation still passes 26 of 26 cases.


7. CURRENT PRODUCTS IN THE MARKET (GENERAL PICTURE)
---------------------------------------------------
Names and features change often, so check the vendors' own pages before quoting any of this.

  Industrial and building energy management platforms
    - Schneider Electric EcoStruxure (Power Monitoring Expert, Resource Advisor)
    - Siemens (SIMATIC Energy Manager; Siemens cloud energy and sustainability tools)
    - ABB Ability Energy Manager
    - Rockwell Automation FactoryTalk Energy Manager
    - Honeywell Forge, Johnson Controls OpenBlue (mainly buildings)
    These collect meter data, show dashboards, trends, alarms and reports, and some add
    energy-performance indicators per unit of production.

  Utility bill and sustainability reporting software
    - EnergyCAP (utility bill accounting), IBM Envizi, Microsoft Sustainability Manager
    These focus on bills, carbon accounting and ESG reporting across many sites.

  Measurement and verification (M&V) tools and standards
    - OpenEEmeter (open source, LF Energy), which implements the CalTRACK methods for
      weather-normalised savings
    - RETScreen (Natural Resources Canada)
    - Standards: IPMVP (EVO), ASHRAE Guideline 14, ISO 50001 / ISO 50006 (energy baselines and
      performance indicators) / ISO 50015 (M&V)
    These define how to build a baseline and prove savings, which is the same idea our baseline uses.

  AI copilots
    Several vendors now add chat assistants on top of their dashboards. In general they summarise
    data in natural language. Publicly documented guarantees that every number in the answer
    comes from a calculation are uncommon.


8. RESEARCH GAPS (WHERE THIS KIND OF SYSTEM CAN STILL IMPROVE)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Exact, explainable bill bridges. Most tools show trends. Few split a bill change into
     price, volume, product mix, weather and waste that add up exactly and do not depend on the
     order the causes are listed in.
  2. Product-mix-aware baselines. Baselines usually use total production or degree-days only. A
     shift to heavier products then looks like inefficiency. Learning a rate per product family
     needs enough variation in the history and breaks down for brand-new products.
  3. Honest uncertainty. Savings and waste are often shown as single numbers. Ranges that are
     both well calibrated and narrow enough to be useful are still an open problem, especially
     with few quarters of data.
  4. Faults that hide in the history. If a leak starts inside the training window, the baseline
     partly learns it as normal. If it starts in a hot quarter, weather can absorb it. Better
     change-point detection and "non-routine event" handling (an IPMVP topic) are needed.
  5. Causation, not correlation. Regression says what moves together, not what caused what.
     Interaction effects (hot weather AND high volume) are ignored by a linear model.
  6. Trustworthy LLM answers. Language models can invent or round numbers. Systems need checks
     like ours (every number matched to a computed fact), and better ways to check meaning, not
     only numbers.
  7. Benchmarks with ground truth. Real factories never know the "true" split. Public test
     datasets with a known answer for bill decomposition and waste attribution are scarce, so
     most evaluation uses synthetic data, which is optimistic.
  8. Sub-meter gaps and calibration. Sub-meters rarely add up to the main meter, and drift over
     time. Robust ways to reconcile them and attribute the unmetered part are still developing.
  9. Whole bills. Demand charges, power-factor penalties, taxes and fixed fees are not in this data,
     and most decomposition methods ignore them.
 10. Small data. One factory with a year or two of history gives a fragile baseline. Transfer
     learning across similar sites is a promising but open area.


9. SHORT GLOSSARY
-----------------
  kWh            energy (kilowatt-hour)
  pp             percentage points of last quarter's bill
  Baseline       the expected energy from production and weather, learned from history
  CDD / HDD      cooling / heating degree-days: how hot or cold each day was
  CV(RMSE)       typical prediction error as a percent of the average (lower is better)
  NMBE           average bias of the predictions (close to 0 is good)
  Idle load      energy used while the production lines are stopped
  Unmetered      main meter minus the sum of sub-meters
  Trace          the saved record of every step behind one answer (GET /trace/{run_id})
